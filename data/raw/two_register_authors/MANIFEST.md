# Two-register authors: the same writer in scripture register and in ordinary prose

Collected 2026-11-15. Purpose: known-answer validation of whether an author stays recognisable across the shift from plain prose into King-James-style "scripture register". The project previously had one such author (Joseph Smith: letters vs revelations). This directory adds others.

**How it was fetched.** `scripts/fetch_two_register_authors.sh` reads `scripts/two_register_authors_sources.tsv` and downloads each item (Internet Archive OCR text + metadata JSON via `scripts/ia_get.sh`; Project Gutenberg and centerplace.org by URL). Files are kept exactly as served. Nothing has been cleaned.

**Where the body text is.** `scripts/two_register_authors_bounds.tsv` gives, for each usable file, the 1-based inclusive line range of the body in the raw file (front matter, editors' introductions, indexes and adverts fall outside the range). `scripts/two_register_authors_counts.py` applies those ranges and writes `body_bounds_counts.tsv` here (word count, a rough OCR indicator, and per-1000-word rates of archaic pronouns, *unto*, *behold/lo*, *-eth* verbs, *came to pass*). **Inside the ranges, running heads, page numbers, verse numbers and footnotes are still present**; the caveats column says where footnotes or interleaved notes are by another hand or in another register.

**Word counts** below are whitespace tokens inside the body range, so they include running heads and notes; treat them as upper bounds (typically 3–10% high, more where noted).

**OCR judgment** comes from reading samples plus `dict_share` (share of tokens found in the system dictionary; proper names and archaic spellings depress it). Roughly: ≥0.95 good, 0.92–0.95 fair, <0.92 poor. All Internet Archive `*.djvu.txt` files are uncorrected OCR with line-end hyphenation. The `bim_eighteenth-century_*` scans keep the long s (ſ) and are the noisiest.

**Licence.** Every work was published before 1931 and is in the US public domain, with the exceptions flagged in the tables (the 1961 edition of Strang's diary; site transcriptions).

**Honest tally.** Eleven pairs clear 10k words on both sides. Of these, **six are strong KJV pastiche** (White, Snowden, Strang, Thompson, Ilive, Memminger), three are moderate (O'Connor, Newbrough, Dunsany) and two are weak (Smith III, Macpherson). Only Dunsany and the Strang/Smith III scripture sides are clean transcriptions; everything else is uncorrected OCR. The cleanest strong pairs for a first experiment are **White**, **Thompson**, **Memminger** and **Strang**; Snowden is strong but needs the Robertson-derived half excluded.

## D. Not tried, worth a look

- **Aleister Crowley** (Holy Books of Thelema, 1907–11, vs essays and *Confessions*): US public domain for pre-1931 printings; texts are scattered across IA uploads of uneven provenance.
- **Joseph Smith III**: more plain prose (memoirs serialised in the *Saints' Herald*, signed editorials) would lift the prose side well above 14k.
- **John Taylor** and **Wilford Woodruff**: uncanonised revelations (1880s) vs large plain-prose output; the revelations total only a few thousand words each.
- **Evans-TCP / ECCO-TCP** keyed transcriptions would give clean text for Snowden, Leacock, Ilive and Dodsley in place of the long-s OCR; not checked.
