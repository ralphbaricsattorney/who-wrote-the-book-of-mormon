# Joseph Smith Papers — passages in Joseph Smith's own hand, 1833–1844 (not previously downloaded)

Selection: every JSP Documents-series item 1827–June 1844 (year listings `/the-papers/documents/{pre1830,1830,…,1844}` plus their "Appendix Items" sub-listings) and every Journals-series item (`/the-papers/journals/jsppj1`, `jsppj2` and the jsppj2 appendix), minus everything already in `data/raw/jsp_*` or in `scripts/jsp_uris_smith_early.txt` / `scripts/jsp_uris_cowdery.txt` — **4,141 candidates**. Each was screened on page 1 only; **51 kept** and fetched in full. URI list: `scripts/jsp_uris_smith_later.txt`. Every candidate with its JSP handwriting statement and keep/skip decision: `screening.tsv`. Composition judgments: `composition_notes.tsv`.

For private analysis only. Content © Intellectual Reserve, Inc. (The Church Historian's Press). Do not redistribute.

### Which texts are in Smith's own hand
- **Holograph letters (the main yield):** Letters to Emma Smith of 19 Aug 1836, 4 Nov 1838, 12 Nov 1838, 1 Dec 1838, 21 Mar 1839, 4 Apr 1839, 9 Nov 1839 and 20–25 Jan 1840; the 18 Aug 1842 letter to the Whitneys; Letter to William Clayton, 7 Oct 1842; Letter to Newel K. Whitney, 12 Dec 1840; Blessing to Sarah Ann Whitney, 23 Mar 1843; Statement and Blessing, ca. Feb 1840; Note to Newel K. Whitney, ca. Oct 1833–early 1834.
- **Journal, 1835–1836:** 765 words in JS's hand (seven entries, about four MS pages, the last JS journal with his own writing); the remaining ~47,000 words are by Warren Parrish and other scribes and are not JS's hand, though many entries were dictated.
- **Egyptian Alphabet–A (ca. Jul–Nov 1835):** ~530 words in JS's hand. It is a list of characters, "sounds" and definitions, not prose; it may need to be excluded from function-word stylometry.
- **Short administrative and legal pieces in JS's hand:** three city-council motions (5 Mar 1842), charges against Harrison Sagers, a warrant, a land agreement, an authorization, notes, and about fifteen promissory notes, pay orders and receipts. These are formulaic, with 6–150 words each.
- **Minimal JS hand in otherwise scribal or co-written documents:** the Liberty jail general epistles of 20 and ca. 22 Mar 1839 (dictated by JS and copied by McRae and Baldwin; JS hand = a few insertions), Letter to David Hale (1841; JS's addition to Lorenzo Wasson's letter), Letter to Emma 26–27 Jun 1844 (dictated to Willard Richards; JS's postscript), the 14 Nov 1842 ordinance, the Tompkins petition (Mar 1839), Report of Committee (Feb 1841), and subscriptions for Willard Richards.
- **Screened but not fetched:**
  - Journal Mar–Sep 1838, Sep–Oct 1838, 1839, Dec 1841–Dec 1842, and Dec 1842–Jun 1844 (Books 1–4). Their statements do not name JS. JSP says these Missouri and Nauvoo journals were neither written nor dictated by JS (scribes George W. Robinson, James Mulholland, William Clayton, Willard Richards and others).
  - 420 documents carrying only JS's signature or minor marks, and 16 more that JSP mentions only for a JS docket, endorsement or notation.
  - Five duplicates. The Vision (16 Feb 1832), Revelation 7 Mar 1832 and Revelation 6 Aug 1833 are the same Revelation Book 2 pages already in `../jsp_revelation_book_2_and_doctrine_and_covenants/`. The Account of Meeting (24 Sep 1835) and Blessing to Newel K. Whitney (7 Oct 1835) are pages of Journal 1835–1836, which was fetched here in full.
- **Warning — JS hand ≠ JS composition:** the "composition" column below separates what JS composed from what he only corrected or copied. In the Tompkins petition and the Brassfield note, JS's few words are corrections to texts drafted by others ("other"). In the 1842 ordinance, his words are revisions inside a council draft ("joint"). The poem to Barbara Neff plays on a couplet W. W. Phelps wrote just above it. No item fetched here is a wholesale copy by JS of another person's letter or revelation, unlike the Cowdery letters in Letterbook 1 in the earlier set.

## How the JSP data were obtained
- **Site and endpoint:** same as `../jsp_joseph_smith_early_writings/MANIFEST.md`. Pages come from the Next.js JSON at `/_next/data/<buildId>/paper-summary/<uri>/<page>.json`. Listings come from `/_next/data/<buildId>/the-papers/documents/<year>.json` (`pageProps.docDetails.panes[2:]`, where `href` = `/paper-summary/<uri>` gives a document and `uri` gives a sub-listing such as `documents/1835/jsppd4-sub01` "Appendix Items") and from `/the-papers/journals/jsppj1|jsppj2[/jsppj2-sub01].json`. Listing parser: `scripts/jsp_parse_listing.py`.
- **Fetched:** 2026-11-17. Single connection, at least 1.2 s between requests, generic browser User-Agent, no images. About 30 listing requests, 4,141 page-1 screening requests and 332 further document-page requests. A handful of 504s and timeouts were retried by the fetcher. Every one of the 51 documents was fetched with no failed pages.
- **Screening** (`scripts/jsp_screen.py`, which loads `scripts/jsp_fetch.py` unchanged and fetches page 1 only; decisions in `scripts/jsp_screen_decide.py`):
  - **Keep** if the JSP handwriting statement names JS as a hand, or if page 1 has at least 20 words in the `josephswriting` class. A statement such as "Handwriting of X and JS", "Handwriting of JS", "…with insertions by JS" or "…additions in the handwriting of JS" names JS as a hand.
  - **Skip** if JS appears only as a signature, signature block, docket, endorsement or notation.
  - When the statement was truncated ("Handwriting of William…") or missing ("No handwriting information was found."), the handwriting clauses of the JSP source note were used instead.
  - Borderline cases were checked by hand.
  - The trimmed page-1 JSON from screening was reused as `raw/<uri>/p0001.json`, so it was not fetched twice. `scripts/jsp_fetch.py` then fetched the remaining pages into this directory.
- **Outputs and scribe attribution:** file layout and attribution method are as in the early-writings manifest (`text/<uri>.txt`, `.pages.jsonl`, `.by_scribe/Joseph_Smith_Jr..txt`, `.meta.json`). "Words in JS hand" = `words_by_scribe["Joseph Smith Jr."]`, i.e. text inside JSP's `josephswriting` spans, with page labels stripped. It includes JS's signatures inside those documents.
- **Caveats:**
  - Screening relies on JSP's statement and page 1. A document whose statement omits JS and whose JS hand is only on later pages would be missed. JSP statements normally list every hand except signatures.
  - The Revelations and Translations, Administrative, Histories, Legal and Financial series were not screened (outside this task), except where items also appear in the Documents listings. JSP describes the large History 1838–1856 and the Nauvoo journals as scribal.
  - Dates and years below are JSP's document dates. Journal 1835–1836 is dated Sep 1835, and its JS entries fall in Sep–Dec 1835.
